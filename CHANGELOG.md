# Changelog

Notable changes to this project, newest first. Format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/); versioning follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

Nothing is released yet. Until the first public build (Phase 6 — see [`Docs/PROJECT_PLAN.md`](Docs/PROJECT_PLAN.md) Section 6), everything lands under **Unreleased**, and the project stays at 0.x with no compatibility promise of any kind — including for the save format.

## [Unreleased]

### Fixed

- **The vertical slice no longer ends 8.5 m underground.** What "dropping through the floor after the loop" actually was: the layout ended 8.498 m below its own station and ran its entire back half below ground, with the rail centreline bottoming out at **−9.60 m**. It had been worked around by raising the actor in the level rather than fixed. `TrackClose.h`'s `HeightTarget` with the lift climb freed solves it in 2 iterations — 55.0 m → **75.11 m**, ending at +0.0007 m. **It costs nothing in ride feel, and that is not luck:** the chain hauls the train to the crest at 4 m/s whatever the crest's height, and the drop geometry below it never moved, so top speed (100.9 km/h), both vertical G peaks (+0.70..+4.25), peak lateral (0.36) and the loop apex (+1.25) all come out bit-identical to the 55 m version. Only the height moved. The lift length is now one named constant used twice, because the chain zone's extent is derived from where the climb ends and a second literal could have drifted out of step and stranded the train short of the crest.

### Added

- **The train has length** (`FTrainConfig::TrainLength`). `PHASE0_FINDINGS.md` called a point-mass train "the one omission most likely to be *felt*", and predicted the fix would be an average over sample points along the train — which is what it is, nine of them. The substitution that matters is one line: the gravity term reads the change in the train's **mean** height rather than its centre's, because a rigid body on rails is accelerated by the slope its whole mass sits on. Measured: a point mass crests at **19.619 m/s** where a 15 m train arrives at **19.735 m/s**, because straddling the crest its mass sits below the crest and it has not paid the full height. Across an asymmetric crest the spread front-to-back is **0.73 g** — front car −1.996 g, centre −2.318 g, back car −2.723 g. `TrainLength = 0` stays the default and collapses every sample to one, so every result measured before this is bit-identical, asserted exactly rather than within a tolerance. `GetForcesAt(offset)` reports what any car feels; `GetFrontS`/`GetRearS` are there for the block system to use later. **One condition the findings did not state: the back-car effect requires an *asymmetric* hill** — on a symmetric one the front and back crest at the same height and there is no difference at all, which the first version of the test correctly measured. Both cases are asserted, the symmetric one as a control.
- **The whole ride, measured at edit time** (`Prototypes/TrainPhysics/RideProfile.h`), drawn as toggleable traces along the track. Runs the train once over the finished layout and records speed, all three felt-G axes, roll rate and height, sampled by **arc length** rather than by time so the data does not thin out exactly where the ride is fastest and the G is worth looking at. Extremes carry *where*: "4.25 g" is a number, "4.25 g at S=310 m" is somewhere to go and look. The single most useful thing it reports is `bCompleted` — an author needs to know a hill is too tall **before** watching a train fail to crest it, and the only way to know is to run it. Each channel draws as a curve offset from the heartline along the rider's own up-vector, so the track is the zero line and through an inversion the trace goes with the rider. Roll rate is a first-class channel because no G trace can ever show it: felt G models the rider as a point, and spinning a point costs nothing. Per-channel visibility and one scale for now — the panel with units and readouts is a UI/UX design question and wants designing rather than accreting.
- **The track is editable.** `Source/TrackUnlimited/TUTrackSegment.h` reflects the authored segment into Unreal, and `ATUCoasterRide` now holds a `TArray<FTUTrackSegment>` seeded with the reference layout — so the ride is *data in the Details panel* rather than a sequence of `AddSegment` calls only editable by recompiling. **The Details panel is the numeric entry UI**: it already has typed fields, per-kind visibility via `EditCondition`, add/remove/reorder, copy/paste, multi-select and its own undo, and reimplementing that in Slate would be a great deal of code to arrive back where we started. It also satisfies the first architectural constraint exactly — numbers in a list, no drag handles anywhere near the viewport. Powered track is per segment too (`ETUSegmentZone`), so a lift is however many segments in a row say "Lift" and moving one is an edit rather than a recompile; arc lengths accumulate over the segments the track actually *accepted*, so a rejected degenerate segment cannot silently shift every zone boundary after it. Every edit rebuilds and reports total length, C² continuity, height gap and closest self-approach — each of which was a defect that previously took *riding* the track to find.
- **Undo/redo** (`Prototypes/TrackSpline/TrackHistory.h`). Snapshots, not commands: the command pattern needs every edit to supply a do *and* an inverse, and the failure mode when those disagree is silent corruption found several undos later. A document is an ordered list of small POD structs, so copying it whole costs nothing and cannot be asymmetric. Equality goes through the **save format** rather than a hand-written `operator==` — correct semantics (two documents are the same exactly when they save the same) and it cannot rot, which matters because `Torsion` and `RollMode` were both added to this model after the fact and a field-by-field comparison would have silently ignored them and dropped undo steps. It also makes a field the segment's kind does not use correctly compare equal, since it is not part of the document's meaning. Committing an unchanged document is a no-op, so an editor that fires on every field callback does not fill the stack with steps that undo nothing. Typing "30.5" into a field is one undo step, not five, via a caller-supplied merge key — the UI owns it because only the UI knows when an edit is finished, so there are no timers and no guessing. Dirty state is tracked as a **position** rather than a flag, so undoing back to what is on disk correctly reports clean; if the depth cap discards the saved state it reports dirty forever, because telling someone their work is safe when the evidence was thrown away is the wrong direction to be wrong in.
- **Self-clearance checking** (`AnalyseSelfClearance` in `Prototypes/TrackSpline/TrackValidate.h`), and `ChainCurvature` in `TrackSpline.h`. Nothing in the model could see the track passing through itself, and it took *riding* the vertical slice to find out: its loop is built from pure pitch curvature, which makes it exactly planar, so the ascending and descending legs pass **0.189 m** apart against rails **1.215 m** wide. Every segment individually valid, every joint continuous, every G reading plausible, rider through solid steel. Third member of the same family — felt G cannot see roll rate, the authored list cannot see the roll geometry contributes, and neither can see two parts of the track in the same place. Fixing it needs a lateral component, i.e. torsion, and composing torsion across segments needed `ChainCurvature`: the phase is measured from each segment's own start, so three torsioned segments in a row step at every joint. Measured cost of the fix on the slice, recorded rather than applied because it is a ride-design call: torsion 0.003 opens the gap to **2.39 m** but takes peak lateral G from **0.36 to 0.96** and drops the exit **22.3 m**.
- **Per-axis closure targets** (`FClosureTarget::bMatchX/Y/Z`, and `HeightTarget`). "Closed" is not one question. A circuit wants all three axes; a point-to-point layout — which is most of what exists right now — wants only Z, because a station-to-brake-run ride is not supposed to end back at the station's footprint. Held to a full circuit target the vertical slice reports 265 m of perfectly truthful, entirely useless failure with the one number that matters buried inside it. Convergence is judged on `ActiveError`, over only the axes the target asked about.
- **Circuit closure: measuring the gap, and solving it** (`Prototypes/TrackSpline/TrackClose.h`). `PHASE0_FINDINGS.md` calls endpoint targeting "the one real cost of the representation" — you author curvature and arrive wherever you arrive, where a control-point model gets closure free. `MeasureClosure` reports the gap per axis and gives **height its own field**, because that is the one that does not announce itself: a track ending 8.5 m low still looks closed in plan view, which is how the vertical slice shipped. `SolveClosure` is damped Gauss-Newton over **authored** parameters the caller explicitly frees — so a solved arc keeps the radius the author typed instead of gaining a curvature field that disagrees with it, and nothing moves that the author did not offer. Measured: 9.093 m closes to 3.0e-06 m in 2 iterations and 11 track walks; 20.98 m to 7.0e-06 m in 2. Radius freedoms have their **sign locked and magnitude floored**, because +ve is a left turn and -ve a right one, so crossing zero would reverse a turn and pass through the infinite curvature that produces a silently straight segment. The finite-difference step is deliberately coarse, because the standing "do not finite-difference `EvaluateAt`" warning applies to the solver too. On failure the document is left **unchanged** by default. Two limits are measured rather than fixed: horizontal straights cannot move height at all (8.93 m survives a solve untouched, zero gradient), and freeing one half of a balanced hill breaks the balance that made it level — closing height while keeping the exit level needs coupled freedoms this solver cannot express.
- **The authored data model and a diffable save format** (`Prototypes/TrackSpline/TrackIO.h`). `FAuthoredSegment` stores what the author *typed* — a helix is "radius 20, climbing 15°, 1.5 turns", not the "length 261.6, curvature 0.0467, torsion 0.0125" the integrator runs — and JSON round-trips it. The rule is **store what was typed, never what was derived**, and it is the same argument that justified the torsion field: the approximation a helix would otherwise need costs roughly one segment per metre, rides identically, and contains the radius **nowhere**, so it can never be edited again. Grouping those segments in the UI fixes the row count and not that — to edit the group you must regenerate it, so the group has to store the authored parameters anyway, at which point the expansion is a cache and this stores the parameters alone. Measured on the format itself: a helix radius change is **one line of diff**; the same edit against a stored expansion would rewrite ~600 fitted curvature endpoints, and only if regeneration were bit-deterministic across compilers. Round trip is **exact** — worst position error 0.0 m over a six-kind layout — and re-saving a loaded file is byte-identical, so opening and closing a track cannot churn a diff. **Angles are degrees** — in the file and in `FAuthoredSegment` itself, the only place in the codebase that is not radians. Nobody types `0.26179938779914941`; they type `15`, and a diff that reads `"climbAngleDeg": 15` says what changed where the radian form says nothing. Putting degrees in the *struct* rather than converting in the reader is what keeps the round trip exact: the conversion runs one direction only, in `BuildSegment`, so degrees → radians → degrees never happens and cannot lose an ulp. Field and key names carry the unit so a hand edit cannot get it wrong. Curvature stays 1/m, because an easement out of a straight has an endpoint with no radius, and curvature 0 says that as an ordinary value where radius would need infinity. Defaults are omitted rather than written, which is what let `Torsion` and `RollMode` both be added to an existing model without touching a stored line. Unknown *keys* load and are ignored (forward compatibility); an unknown segment **kind** or a future format version is refused outright, because silently skipping geometry leaves a file that loads, looks fine, and is a different ride. 11 malformed inputs rejected with a reason, and the writer refuses to emit a non-finite value rather than write `nan`.
- **World-referenced roll, as a per-segment mode** (`ERollMode` in `Prototypes/TrackSpline/TrackSpline.h`). `PHASE0_FINDINGS.md` recorded that `Roll = 0` does not mean level with the horizon on non-planar track, and left "keep rotation-minimising" versus "add a world-referenced mode" open as a Phase 1 decision. The answer is both, per segment, because neither is defined everywhere a coaster goes: world bank has no meaning pointing straight up, and path-relative roll makes the author compute their own banking. A corner hill authored at roll 0 exits **54.736°** off level path-relative and **5.7e-14** world-referenced. Resolved per sample at walk time rather than baked in when typed, so editing an upstream hill cannot silently unbank a turn downstream; `FTrackFrame::Roll` stays path-relative, so felt G, the cross-section and the ride camera needed no changes at all. Cheap because roll never perturbs the path — it is applied last, as a rotation about the tangent, making the solve one `atan2` against a frame already in hand.
- **`AnalyseResolvedRollRate`** (`Prototypes/TrackSpline/TrackValidate.h`), which walks the built track rather than reading the authored list — and closes a second blind spot found while adding the mode above. A segment holding a constant world bank through a climbing turn types **0.0 rad/m** of roll rate and rotates the rider the whole way, at a measured **0.0296 rad/m** (34.0 °/s, 0.018 g of head snap), because the path frame twists underneath the constant number. Felt G cannot see roll rate; the segment list cannot see geometry. It also covers world bank's failure mode without a special case: the level reference flips through the vertical, so holding a bank over the top of a loop measures **12.6 rad/m** against **0.0** for identical geometry authored path-relative.
- **Vertical slice** (`Source/TrackUnlimited/TUCoasterRide.cpp`). The first code to take the prototypes into the engine, and the last Phase 0 engineering item. A hand-authored 543.7 m layout — station, eased 25° lift, crest, drop, teardrop loop, banked turn, brake run — authored numerically as an ordered list of typed segment parameters, with no viewport dragging anywhere. `Build.cs` puts `Prototypes/` on the public include path and includes the headers **directly** rather than copying them, so the standalone assert suites test exactly the code that ships. Builds clean against UE 5.8.1 with zero warnings, and in-engine `BuildTrack` logs the same `16 segments, 543.7 m, curvature-continuous=yes` the standalone harness produces. Cart rides the rail centreline, camera rides the heartline, so the slice shows the distinction rather than collapsing it.
- **NoLimits 2 CSV import/export prototype** (`Prototypes/NL2Csv/`). Reads and writes NL2's documented tab-separated spline export, so real NL2 layouts can be driven through the model and ours opened in NL2 for comparison. Reconstruction fits curvature segments from sampled frames by decomposing each interval's Darboux vector against a parallel-transported path frame. Round-trips a 529.8 m layout to 2.6 mm worst position and 6.8e-04 g rms felt-G, with a full-circuit train speed divergence of 1.7e-03 m/s. Validated against a real 466-sample NL2 export. 13 assert-based tests.
- **NoLimits 2 telemetry prototype** (`Prototypes/NL2Telemetry/`). The NL2 telemetry wire protocol as an engine-free, socket-free header (testable offline), plus a live recorder that correlates a running ride against an imported track. Telemetry ships in the **standard** NL2 edition, unlike the Professional-only CSV export, so this path works regardless of licence tier. 7 assert-based tests against hand-built bytes.
- **Train physics prototype** (`Prototypes/TrainPhysics/`). 1D train motion constrained to the track, standalone C++17. Gravity is applied as an exact energy exchange rather than an integrated force, so a frictionless circuit conserves energy to 5e-14 m/s across a 333× range of timestep. Rolling resistance follows the normal load from the track's felt G, air drag is a lumped `v²` term, and one zone shape covers lift chain, tyre launch, brake run and station — they differ only in target speed and in how hard they may push or hold back. Reports fore-aft G alongside the track's lateral and vertical. 18 assert-based tests, 22 of 22 mutants killed.
- **Track spline prototype** (`Prototypes/TrackSpline/`). Curvature-continuous track geometry as standalone C++17 with no engine dependency. Segments are curvature profiles over arc length — straight, constant-radius arc, clothoid transition — integrated into geometry by an exponential-midpoint scheme that keeps the frame orthonormal by construction. Heartline-relative banking, rail centreline derivation, and felt lateral/vertical G. 14 assert-based tests.
- **Block signalling prototype** (`Prototypes/BlockSignal/`). The `CLEAR → OCCUPIED → BUFFER(x) → CLEAR` per-block state machine with configurable safety overlap, plus dispatch permissive logic with wrapping multi-block lookahead. Standalone C++17, 7 assert-based tests.
- **`Docs/PHASE0_FINDINGS.md`** — what the prototypes proved (with numbers), what was disproved, the known-limitations ledger, and the measured UE5 port checklist.
- UE5 project skeleton: `TrackUnlimited.uproject`, `Config/`, and an empty `TrackUnlimited` runtime module.
- This changelog.

### Changed

- **Phase 4's procedural meshing layer will be built, not adapted — the Coaster Forge evaluation is closed, and with it the last Phase 0 gate.** `PROJECT_PLAN.md` Section 8 called licensing/adapting existing marketplace work "the single biggest lever available to compress a multi-year solo effort", but that lever does not exist here: Coaster Forge is a commercial product and cannot be redistributed under MIT, which the repo's own constraint on paid marketplace assets already implied. The purchase would have bought reference reading followed by an original implementation regardless. Two things also moved since the plan was written — the zone-based speed control cited as the pattern worth borrowing was arrived at independently and verified against NoLimits 2 (`FTrackZone`), and a cross-section specification now exists (`Prototypes/TrackSpline/TrackProfile.h`), so Phase 4 begins from typed dimensions rather than a blank sheet. What remains there is technique, chiefly support/footer auto-placement, which no prior art was going to de-risk.
- **The lateral-G sign now matches a running copy of NoLimits 2, and the documentation reading that contradicted it was wrong.** `PHASE0_FINDINGS.md` flagged this as "one negation now and expensive later" and it turns out to be **no negation at all**: across a recorded lap, sign agreement is 627/627 samples wherever NL2's lateral exceeds 0.3 g. Magnitude agreement is far tighter than the sign check needed — 0.0089 g rms vertical and 0.0299 g rms lateral over 4,298 samples, with the peak unbanked turn reading +2.3521 g in NL2 against +2.3979 g in the model. NL2 was also shown to report G in the **car's local frame** (0.909 on the lift against cos(pitch) = 0.914, versus 1.000 for world) and as the force felt **by the rider**, both decided from data rather than from the docs, which specify neither.
- **`RollingResistance` and `DragK` have their first real measurement.** Fitted to NL2's own speed trace over 1,700 coasting samples, the shipped defaults run that ride 0.998 m/s rms fast; 0.0046 / 0.00144 halves it to 0.564. **The defaults are deliberately unchanged** — one train on one 233 m layout, and the fitted `DragK` lands 3.2× above its physically derived value, which is what modelling a 10–15 m train as a point at the heartline would look like. Recorded as evidence for the train-length limitation, not as a new default.
- **Track representation is curvature-profile-over-arc-length, superseding the cubic Hermite / B-spline model** originally specified in `PROJECT_PLAN.md` Section 5. C² continuity becomes a property of the data rather than something fitted, and clothoids are the native case rather than special-cased. Rationale in `Prototypes/TrackSpline/TrackSpline.h`; consequences in `Docs/PHASE0_FINDINGS.md`.
- `PROJECT_PLAN.md` Section 6 no longer describes the Phase 1 editor as having "draggable handles" — that contradicted the project's first architectural constraint, and the data model has no control points to drag.
- The segment vocabulary now records that **a helix is not expressible as a linear curvature profile** and needs its own segment type. Authored naively it produces a flat tilted circle, not a helix.
- `README.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md` and `CLAUDE.md` updated for a repo that now contains working code, including build instructions for the prototypes and the UE 5.8 engine pin.

### Fixed

- **Powered sections could manufacture energy on a gradient.** A zone was a per-*time* clamp on speed applied after a per-*distance* energy exchange, so on a slope the two ratcheted against each other: a lift chain rated at 3.0 m/s² — physically unable even to hold the train against a 30° grade's 4.9 — climbed 19.5 m anyway, inventing 191 J/kg out of nothing at every timestep tested. Zones are now tractive accelerations resolved before the energy step, so `MaxAccel` means what its comment always claimed. An underpowered lift now correctly fails to move the train, and an adequate one holds chain speed uphill at exactly `sin(grade)` G fore-aft.
- **A train at rest on a gradient never moved.** Position was integrated from entry speed alone, so zero speed meant zero travel, meant no height change, meant zero speed — forever. Placing a cart at the top of a drop, the vertical slice's most obvious first experiment, produced nothing at all. Position now carries the acceleration term, and a train released from rest reaches `sqrt(2g·Δz)` at every grade tested.
- Fore-aft G mixed conventions: lateral and vertical were apparent G, but tangential returned raw `dv/dt`, putting phantom load on every slope in the ride. A freely rolling train now reads exactly 0 G, as it should — gravity is accelerating it, not pushing against it.
- A train coming to rest could creep at ~1e-9 m/s forever, because `S1 - S0` is not bit-identical to the computed advance once the distance travelled is dwarfed by the position.
- `FTrain::AddZone` now rejects malformed zones rather than storing them — an inverted span silently never fires, and a negative `MaxDecel` turned a brake run into an unbounded launch.
- Path acceleration was resolved onto the *banked* lateral axis instead of the path's, which would have left a banked turn never quite cancelling its own lateral G — wrong in a way that throws no error.
- Roll sign was inverted: a right-hand rotation about the tangent drops the rider's right side, so `+bank` leaned *out* of a left turn.
- `FTrack::AddSegment` now rejects zero-length, negative and NaN segment lengths. A zero-length segment whose endpoint curvatures matched its neighbours could otherwise satisfy `IsCurvatureContinuous` at both joints while bridging a real discontinuity — a measured 0.76 G lateral step reported as continuous.
- `FBlockController::CanDispatch` denies a zero lookahead. It previously returned an unconditional dispatch permissive that checked nothing — verified across every state vector for N ≤ 6, including with every block occupied.
- `FBlockController::Tick` ignores zero, negative and NaN deltas. A NaN would latch a block in `BUFFER` permanently with no later well-formed tick able to free it; a negative delta grew a countdown past its configured ceiling.
- Test coverage on the vertical axis, found by mutation testing: four broken variants of the spline header passed the original suite, including one that inverted the entire track (loop apex at z = -16 instead of +16) while leaving every G reading numerically identical. All previously-surviving mutants are now killed.

### Security

- Removed a generated `AndroidFileServer` `SecurityToken` from `Config/DefaultEngine.ini` before it reached history. This is a PC-first project with no Android target, so the whole section was dead weight.
- `ModelContextProtocol` is now restricted to `Editor` targets in the `.uproject`. It is marked `NoRedist` with unrestricted runtime modules, so it would otherwise compile into a packaged Shipping build.
